\begin{abstract}\noindent
We combine Parts I--V, which study a generalized-geometry version of the three treasures of complex geometry (Riemann--Roch, Serre vanishing, Kodaira embedding) starting from the simplest nontrivial example: the generalized complex structure of jumping type obtained by a $\beta$-transform of a holomorphic Poisson structure $\sigma$, and generalized holomorphic line bundles $L$ on it.
(1) A generalized holomorphic structure is a Poisson module; if $\sigma$ is log symplectic it is a flat logarithmic connection whose residues $r_i$ are constants.
(2) Serre-type vanishing holds in the form of ``vanishing in transversal degree''; vanishing in total degree fails. The duality $\HH^k(L)^*\cong\HH^{2n-k}(L^\vee\otimes K^2)$ holds in every dimension.
(3) The total Euler characteristic is $(-1)^n\rk(E)\chi(X)$ and independent of $c_1$. For surfaces and toric varieties we give the Poincar\'e polynomial in terms of residues, and we prove the local formula for quasi-homogeneous singularities.
(4) In the regular type the dimensions are polynomial in $m$ degree by degree, in the log symplectic type they are periodic. In general dimension the reduction theorem reduces everything to a condition (GEN$'$).
(5) For Kodaira-type statements, an embedding by generalized holomorphic sections is impossible; we prove the leaf-space version and a toric Poisson--Kodaira theorem.
The generalization of the index theorem (weighted b-calculus) remains a conjecture, and a general generalized Kodaira embedding theorem is open.
\end{abstract}
